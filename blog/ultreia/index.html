LOVE WINS ERA · THINK ABOUT THIS

Ultreia.

Sat, 18 Apr 2026

The biggest reasons your journey succeeds… or doesn’t 🥾🥾 👇 Most people think a long-distance walk is purely physical. But it rarely is. What determines whether you finish… sits somewhere else. In your mind. And that’s where it usually goes wrong. 1. Ultreia — leave little room for doubt Most people start… but doubt along the way. You see it. In small sentences. In small decisions. Before every journey, I tell myself: As long as I don’t get sick, don’t get injured, and my budget is okay… I will make it. No doubt. No negotiation. Most people never truly make that agreement with themselves. 2. The people around you matter more than you think You start alone… but you rarely walk alone. And not everyone moves you forward. Some people carry you. Others slowly drain you. You only notice it when your energy is gone. 3. Money disappears faster than you think. This is where things often go quiet. People don’t like talking about it. But many journeys end here. Not because of the walking. But because of the money. A coffee here. An extra night there. And suddenly, you have to stop. 4. Listen to your body The first signals are small. A little pain. A tension. Fatigue that doesn’t go away. Most people walk through it. Until they can’t. This is not a sprint. This is a long-distance journey. 5. Ready to quit? Stop. But not permanently There comes a moment when everything comes together. Bad weather. Fatigue. Doubt. And quitting starts to feel logical. But often, it’s not the end. It’s just exhaustion. Check in somewhere. Stay a few days. And see what happens. 6. Be careful with the story you tell yourself There’s one sentence you often hear right before someone quits: “The path has given me what I needed.” Sometimes that’s true. But often… it’s just a beautiful way to give up. Not everything that sounds right, is right. Be aware of your thinking. 7. The weight of your backpack determines your journey After a week, you already know. What do you really use? What are you carrying “just in case”? Many people carry their doubt literally. Too much weight. Too many “maybes.” This is something I learned on the Appalachian Trail: After one week of walking, make three piles: A) used every day B) used occasionally C) never used That last pile? Send it home. The middle one… that’s usually where the problem is. Most journeys don’t fail because of strength. They fail somewhere along the way… without anyone really seeing it happen. 👉 At what point did things almost turn for you… or actually did? 🥾🥾 Steven “Indiana” Huysseune #LongDistanceWalking #HikingMindset #TrailLessons
