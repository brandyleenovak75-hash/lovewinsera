Intimacy Without Belonging

There is a particular kind of heartbreak that comes from being deeply loved in private and strangely absent from someone's actual life. It can take a long time to recognize because the intimacy is real. That's what makes it so confusing. The tenderness is real. The way someone holds you can be real. The conversations at two in the morning can be real. The concern can be real. The sex can be real. The way they look at you, reach for you, sleep beside you, worry about you, ask you not to hurt them, and make you feel for a few hours like there is nowhere else either of you could possibly belong can all be completely real. And still, somehow, you don't belong. I think I finally have a name for that. Intimacy without belonging.

There Is a Difference Between Being Loved and Being Included I used to think those things naturally traveled together. If someone loved you, surely they would make room for you. Not every room. Not every minute. Not unrestricted access to every corner of another person's existence. Love doesn't eliminate privacy or autonomy. But surely there would be a place for you. An ordinary one. That's the part I kept wanting. Not some grand declaration. Not possession. Not constant attention. I wanted the mundane parts. Bring my laptop over. Sit at the table. You do what you need to do. I'll do what I need to do. Maybe we barely speak for three hours. Maybe you work outside while I answer emails. Maybe one of us makes coffee. Maybe we eventually look up from whatever we're doing and realize we've spent an entire afternoon together without actually doing anything together. That sounds incredibly unromantic. I think it might actually be one of the most intimate things there is. Because there is a difference between being invited into someone's moments and being incorporated into their life. I don't need someone to abandon his life for me. I want someone who lets me belong in it.,

If you need me, I will be 42 Degrees From Here.

Brandy Lee Novak
