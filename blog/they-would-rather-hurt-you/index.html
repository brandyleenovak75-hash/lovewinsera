They Would Rather Hurt You

Sat, 26 Sep 2026

They Would Rather Hurt You Than Be Accountable

Some people would rather destroy a relationship than admit they were the ones holding the match.

It's always the people you thought would never do you like that. Every.

Damn.

Time.

The ones who tell you they love you.

The ones who call themselves your friends.

The ones who know your history, your wounds, your fears, and all the shit you fought your way through just to be able to trust somebody again. The ones who looked you in the face and made you believe you were safe with them.

And then they turn around and do the very things they knew would hurt you.

And the worst part?

They don't just hurt you.

They make you regret ever believing they wouldn't.

Now you're sitting there, trying to understand how someone who claimed to love you could knowingly put you through something they wouldn't want done to themselves.

How do they sleep at night? Do they even feel anything? Do they understand what they've done?

And how in the actual fuck did YOU end up being the problem for having a problem with what THEY did?.

Because apparently, expecting people to take responsibility for their own behavior is asking too much these days. Apparently, the real crime isn't hurting someone. It's making the person who hurt you uncomfortable by bringing it up.

And some people will burn an entire relationship to the ground before they'll admit they were holding the match.

1. They Can Feel Bad and Still Refuse to Be Accountable

Here's something that took me far too long to understand. Feeling guilty doesn't mean someone gives enough of a damn to do anything about it. They can feel bad. They can miss you. They can cry about losing you. They can tell everyone how much they love you and how devastated they are that things turned out this way.

But ask them to acknowledge what they actually DID?

Suddenly, nobody knows what you're talking about. Suddenly, they need time. Suddenly, you're being unreasonable. Suddenly, they have a thousand explanations for why they couldn't possibly have handled things differently.

Funny how they can find the words to defend themselves but can't seem to locate a simple fucking apology.

Because accountability requires more than feeling uncomfortable about the consequences of your actions.

It requires admitting you were wrong. Not misunderstood. Not unfairly judged. Not the victim of somebody else's reaction. WRONG.

It requires being able to say:

I did that. I hurt you. I had a choice, and I made one that caused you pain. I don't get to erase that just because acknowledging it makes me uncomfortable.

And for some people, that is apparently a bridge too damn far.

They have invested so much energy into believing they're good people that they cannot tolerate evidence of themselves behaving like assholes.

So instead of changing the behavior, they change the story.

They convince themselves they had no choice.

They convince themselves you made them do it.

They convince themselves it wasn't that bad.

They convince themselves that because they didn't intend to hurt you, they shouldn't have to answer for the fact that they did.

And if you refuse to accept their rewritten version of events?

Well, now you're the difficult one.

Some people aren't sorry they hurt you. They're sorry you won't let them pretend they didn't.

And there's a difference.

A BIG fucking difference.

2. They Will Justify Instead of Apologize

This is the part that really pisses me off.

Because the original betrayal is bad enough.

But then you have to survive the absolute circus that follows when you dare to confront them about it.

You approach someone you love and tell them they hurt you.

Not because you want a fight.

Not because you're trying to punish them.

But because you believed the relationship mattered enough to have an uncomfortable conversation.

And what do you get?

A courtroom.

A defense attorney.

A character assassination.

And apparently, you're the defendant.

You misunderstood.

You're too sensitive.

That's not what I meant.

You're making a big deal out of nothing.

Well, you did this other thing six months ago.

I guess I'm just a terrible person, then.

Oh, for fuck's sake.

Nobody asked you to be a terrible person. We asked you to take responsibility for a terrible choice.

Those are not the same thing.

You don't get to turn every conversation about your behavior into a referendum on whether you're a good person.

You don't get to demand that the person you hurt reassure you that you're wonderful before you'll even consider acknowledging their pain.

And you certainly don't get to use your intentions as a get-out-of-accountability-free card.

I didn't mean to hurt you.

Okay.

And?

You still did.

Your intentions might explain your behavior.

They do not erase its impact.

You can have a bad day and still owe somebody an apology.

You can be struggling and still be responsible for how you treat people.

You can have trauma, triggers, insecurities, fears, and a whole damn encyclopedia of reasons for your behavior.

And you can still be wrong.

Your pain does not give you permission to create pain for somebody else and then demand understanding instead of accountability.

But some people would rather spend three hours explaining why they did what they did than spend thirty seconds admitting they shouldn't have done it.

They'll argue semantics.

They'll dispute timelines.

They'll bring up your past.

They'll accuse you of attacking them.

They'll pick apart the way you expressed your hurt until the entire conversation becomes about your tone instead of their actions.

And eventually, you're so exhausted from defending the fact that you have feelings that you forget you came there looking for an apology.

That's the second betrayal.

The moment you realize the person who hurt you is more concerned with escaping responsibility than understanding what they put you through.

They would rather make you question your reality than question their behavior.

And that shit is exhausting.

3. They Can Miss You Without Being Willing to Change

Now here's where people really get confused.

Because someone can genuinely miss you and still have absolutely no intention of doing the work required to stop hurting you.

They miss your presence.

They miss your attention.

They miss your loyalty.

They miss having someone who listens, understands, forgives, and keeps showing up.

They miss the comfort of knowing you were there.

But do they miss you enough to examine how they treated you?

Do they miss you enough to have the conversation they've been avoiding?

Do they miss you enough to admit they were wrong without attaching seventeen excuses to the apology?

Do they miss you enough to CHANGE?

Because that's where the answer gets interesting.

Some people don't want reconciliation.

They want their access restored.

They want to come back into your life without having to address why you finally closed the door.

They want to skip the uncomfortable conversation and get straight back to the part where you love them.

They want your kindness without respecting your boundaries.

Your forgiveness without demonstrating change.

Your loyalty without offering the same in return.

And when you refuse to participate in that arrangement anymore, they act like YOU are the one destroying the relationship.

As though the relationship wasn't already being destroyed every time they chose their comfort over your well-being.

As though the damage only became real when you stopped tolerating it.

Let me make something perfectly clear.

You do not get to repeatedly hurt someone and then act betrayed when they finally believe your behavior.

You don't get to demand trust from someone you've given reasons not to trust you.

You don't get to call someone unforgiving because they won't volunteer for another round of the same bullshit.

And you don't get to claim you want things back the way they were when the way things were is exactly what broke them.

Maybe they miss you.

Maybe they love you.

Maybe losing you hurts like hell.

But none of that means they're prepared to love you differently.

And you are not required to keep giving someone opportunities to prove that they haven't changed.

The Part That Makes Me Angry

It's not just what they did.

It's that they KNEW you.

They knew what you'd been through.

They knew how hard you worked to trust again.

They knew what betrayal had already cost you.

They knew the things you were afraid of.

And whether they intended to recreate those wounds or not, they had enough information to understand why their actions would hurt.

But somehow, when you finally react, you're expected to extend all the grace in the world.

Understand their intentions.

Consider their circumstances.

Remember their good qualities.

Give them the benefit of the doubt.

Be the bigger person.

Be patient.

Be forgiving.

Be understanding.

Again.

And again.

And again.

At what fucking point does being the bigger person stop meaning swallowing your own pain to make somebody else comfortable?

Why is the person who was hurt always expected to demonstrate the most emotional maturity?

Why is the person who broke the trust allowed to dictate how quickly it should be restored?

Why does the person who caused the damage get to decide when everybody should move on?

And why, when you finally say ENOUGH, does everybody suddenly become concerned about your attitude?

Where was all that concern when you were the one being hurt?

Where was the demand for compassion when you needed it?

Where was the lecture about love and forgiveness when somebody else had the opportunity to choose kindness and didn't?

I'm tired of the expectation that the person who was hurt must also be the person who makes everybody comfortable with what happened.

Sometimes anger is appropriate.

Sometimes anger is the part of you that finally recognizes that something was unacceptable.

Sometimes anger is what shows up when you've spent too damn long explaining away behavior that deserved to be confronted.

And sometimes you're not angry because you stopped loving someone.

You're angry because you loved them enough to believe they would never treat you like that.

You Don't Get to Hurt Me and Then Tell Me How to Heal

Here's where I am with all of it.

I'm not interested in begging people to understand the damage they've done.

I'm not interested in presenting evidence to someone who has already decided they're innocent.

I'm not interested in arguing with somebody about whether my pain meets their personal standards for being legitimate.

And I am damn sure not interested in making myself smaller so someone else can continue pretending their behavior has no consequences.

You don't have to agree with my feelings for me to have them.

You don't have to approve of my boundaries for me to enforce them.

You don't have to acknowledge the damage for me to recognize that it happened.

And you don't have to apologize for me to decide I'm done participating in it.

I can understand why you did something and still refuse to accept it.

I can recognize that you're human and still hold you responsible for your choices.

I can love you and still recognize that loving you is hurting me.

And I can walk away without needing you to agree that I have a good enough reason.

You do not get to hurt me and then appoint yourself the authority on how much it should hurt.

You do not get to betray my trust and then complain that I no longer trust you.

You do not get to avoid accountability and then accuse me of refusing to move forward.

And you do not get to demand the benefits of a relationship you are unwilling to help make safe.

If you want forgiveness, understand what you're asking to be forgiven for.

If you want trust, behave in ways that make trust possible.

If you want reconciliation, participate in repair.

And if you want to be part of someone's life, stop treating their willingness to love you as permission to keep hurting them.

Because love is not an unlimited supply of second chances.

Love is not a contract requiring someone to tolerate disrespect.

Love is not the absence of boundaries.

And love sure as hell isn't pretending that everything is okay because telling the truth might make somebody uncomfortable.

So, How Do They Sleep at Night?

I don't know.

Maybe they feel guilty.

Maybe they've convinced themselves they did nothing wrong.

Maybe they miss you.

Maybe they don't.

Maybe one day they'll understand.

Maybe they never will.

But I'm done measuring the legitimacy of my pain by somebody else's willingness to acknowledge it.

I'm done confusing someone's regret over losing me with accountability for how they treated me.

And I'm done waiting for people to become who they promised they were.

You can keep your excuses. I'll keep my self-respect.

You can tell whatever version of the story helps you sleep at night.

I know what happened.

I know what it cost me.

And I know I don't have to keep paying for it.

I don't need revenge.

I don't need you to suffer.

I don't even need you to admit I'm right.

But I will not continue abandoning myself to preserve a relationship with someone who refuses to take responsibility for the harm they cause.

You can love someone and still be done with their bullshit.

You can forgive someone and still never give them the same access to you again.

You can wish someone well and still want them nowhere near your peace.

And you can stop carrying the weight of a relationship that somebody else was unwilling to help protect.

I didn't stop loving you because you made a mistake.

I stopped making excuses when you made avoiding accountability more important than repairing what you broke.

And if that makes me the villain in your version of the story?

So be it.

At least I finally stopped betraying myself to keep you comfortable.

---

Love is literally the whole point.

But love without accountability is just another pretty word people use while doing ugly things.

Love is how we win. But not by losing ourselves to people who refuse to take responsibility for how they treat us.
