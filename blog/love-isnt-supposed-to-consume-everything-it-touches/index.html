Love isn't supposed to consume everything it touches.

LOVE IS SUPPOSED TO FEEL SAFE. NOT LIKE SOMETHING YOU HAVE TO SURVIVE. 💜

And I'm not just talking about romantic love.

I'm talking about the kind of love that exists between friends, families, communities, and the people we choose to let into our lives.

The kind of love we give ourselves.

The kind of love that doesn't require you to abandon yourself to receive it.

Somewhere along the way, we started confusing intensity with connection.

We learned to call chaos passion, inconsistency mystery, and emotional exhaustion the price of caring deeply.

We learned to prove our love by how much we could tolerate.

How much we could forgive.

How long we could hold on.

How much of ourselves we were willing to sacrifice to keep someone else comfortable.

And sometimes, we became so good at surviving unhealthy love that healthy love started feeling unfamiliar.

But love was never supposed to be a test of how much pain you could endure.

Safe love doesn't mean love without conflict. It means love where conflict doesn't threaten your humanity.

It means being able to disagree without being punished.

It means having boundaries without being accused of betrayal.

It means being able to say, "That hurt me," without having to prepare a courtroom defense for your feelings.

It means accountability without humiliation.

Honesty without cruelty.

Closeness without control.

And freedom without the constant fear of abandonment.

Safe love gives people room to be human.

To make mistakes.

To change their minds.

To grow beyond the versions of themselves that other people have become comfortable with.

It doesn't demand perfection as the price of belonging.

And it doesn't confuse access to someone with ownership of them

Healthy love doesn't ask you to disappear so someone else can feel secure.

It doesn't require you to shrink your voice, betray your instincts, or make yourself smaller to maintain the peace.

Because peace that depends on your silence isn't peace.

It's an arrangement.

And you deserve relationships where your presence is welcomed, not merely tolerated as long as you remain convenient.

But here's the part we don't talk about enough.

We have to learn how to GIVE safe love, too.

Not just recognize when we're receiving it.

We have to learn to listen without immediately defending ourselves.

To apologize without making the other person responsible for our shame.

To respect boundaries even when they disappoint us.

To stop using our wounds as permission to wound other people.

To recognize that someone else's independence is not automatically a rejection of us.

And to understand that loving someone does not give us the right to decide who they are allowed to become.

Sometimes the most loving thing we can do is stop demanding that other people manage the parts of ourselves we haven't learned to hold.

Love is not the absence of responsibility. Love is one of the reasons responsibility matters.

And none of this happens because we wake up one morning and magically become emotionally healthy people.

We learn.

We practice.

We recognize old patterns.

We make different choices.

We repair what we can.

We release what we cannot.

And we begin creating relationships where people don't have to earn their safety by surrendering themselves.

That's the kind of love I want to see more of in this world.

Not just the kind that looks beautiful in photographs.

The kind that feels safe when nobody is watching.

The kind that shows up in difficult conversations, respected boundaries, honest apologies, and ordinary moments of consideration.

The kind that allows two people to reach toward one another without either one having to lose themselves in the process.

Because love isn't supposed to consume everything it touches.

Love is supposed to create room for life to grow.

And that includes YOUR life.

Your voice.

Your needs.

Your becoming.

Your right to exist as a whole human being.

Love is literally the whole point.

But if we're going to build a Love Wins Era, we have to be willing to redefine what winning with love actually looks like.

Not possession.

Not performance.

Not endurance.

Safety. Respect. Accountability. Freedom. Connection.

Love that makes room for all of us to be human.

Love that begins with how we treat ourselves and extends into how we treat one another.

Love that doesn't require someone to break before we decide they deserve tenderness.

That's the love worth practicing.

That's the love worth building.

And that's the love I believe can change the world.

One move. One moment. One human being at a time.

💜🐦‍🔥 THIS IS THE LOVE WINS ERA.

If you need me, I'll be 42 Degrees From Here.
